
# Primordial Black Holes in Dark Dimensions

### From Quantum Evaporation to Gravitational-Wave Signatures

Research repository for a review of primordial black holes (PBHs) in five- and six-dimensional dark-dimension scenarios, with accompanying LaTeX source, Python/Jupyter calculations, and publication-ready figures.

## Overview

This project investigates how compact extra dimensions modify gravitational physics, black-hole thermodynamics, primordial black-hole evolution, and gravitational-wave signatures.

The research focuses on:

- Five- and six-dimensional dark-dimension models.
- Kaluza–Klein (KK) modes and compactification scales.
- Static and rotating higher-dimensional black holes.
- Hawking radiation and quantum memory-burden effects.
- PBH formation and dark-matter abundance.
- Primordial curvature perturbations.
- Scalar-induced gravitational waves (SIGWs).
- LISA, DECIGO, and BBO detector prospects.
- Numerical calculations and Fisher-matrix forecasts.

## Theoretical Framework

We consider gravitational theories in D = 4 + n spacetime dimensions, where n denotes the number of compact spatial dimensions.

For flat, equal-radius toroidal compactification, the fundamental gravitational scale satisfies:

$$
M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2=(2\pi R)^n M_{4+n}^{n+2}.
$$

The first nonzero Kaluza–Klein mass is:

$$
m_{\mathrm{KK}}=\frac{1}{R}.
$$

We investigate two illustrative scenarios:

- **5D Dark Dimension:** One compact extra dimension.
- **6D Dark Dimensions:** Two compact extra dimensions.

## Primordial Black-Hole Thermodynamics

For a neutral, nonrotating black hole in D spacetime dimensions:

$$
T_H=\frac{D-3}{4\pi r_H}.
$$

The leading semiclassical lifetime scales as:

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{BH}}\propto
M_{\mathrm{BH}}^{(D-1)/(D-3)}.
$$

Phenomenological memory-burden effects can modify the evaporation history and extend black-hole lifetimes.

A longer lifetime does not automatically imply that the PBHs can constitute all dark matter.

## Primordial Curvature Perturbations

The primordial curvature spectrum is parametrized using a log-normal enhancement:

$$
\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}(k)
=
\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{CMB}}(k)
+
\frac{A_{\mathcal R}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_k}
\exp\left[
-\frac{\ln^2(k/k_*)}{2\sigma_k^2}
\right].
$$

Such enhanced perturbations may generate primordial black holes and second-order gravitational waves.

## Scalar-Induced Gravitational Waves

The gravitational-wave energy density is characterized by:

$$
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)
=
\frac{1}{\rho_c}
\frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}}{d\ln f}.
$$

The project considers radiation-era SIGWs generated by enhanced scalar perturbations and compares illustrative signals with the frequency ranges of current and proposed gravitational-wave observatories.

## KK Regimes

Two benchmark regimes are distinguished using the PBH formation Hubble scale:

**Region I — 4D Effective Regime**

$$
H_{\mathrm{form}}<m_{\mathrm{KK}}.
$$

**Region II — Higher-Dimensional Regime**

$$
H_{\mathrm{form}}>m_{\mathrm{KK}}.
$$

These conditions characterize the formation-scale hierarchy. They do not independently establish massive KK graviton production.

## Scientific Limitations

The calculations provide illustrative theoretical benchmarks.

- Memory-burden effects are phenomenological assumptions.
- PBH survival does not guarantee an allowed dark-matter abundance.
- Standard SIGW calculations assume a specified cosmological background.
- Full massive-KK tensor production requires additional calculations.
- Some detector comparisons are illustrative rather than official sensitivity forecasts.
